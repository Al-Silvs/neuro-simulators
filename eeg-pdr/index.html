EEG: posterior dominant rhythm

A simulated awake adult EEG with a scalp voltage map. Pause and step through an alpha cycle to see the field over the head.

Recording

Display: negative up (EEG convention). Sensitivity in µV/mm (1 mm drawn as about 3.8 px). 10 s page, low-frequency filter 1 Hz. Click or drag on the traces to pause and place the cursor; ← → step 4 ms (Shift 40 ms), Space plays or pauses, E opens or closes the eyes.

Scalp voltage at the cursor

−500 µV+50

 

Top view, nose up, left on the left. Red is positive, blue negative (true polarity, unlike the traces). Average reference, spherical-spline interpolation from the 19 electrodes. This is the voltage on the scalp, not on the cortex.

Things to look at

  • One alpha cycle. Pause during a run of alpha and step with →. The occipital maximum changes sign every half cycle (about 50 ms at 10 Hz). The frontal region has the opposite sign, partly because the average reference forces the mean of all electrodes to zero.
  • Double banana. The maximum is at O1/O2, the end of each chain, so there is no phase reversal: the largest deflections are in P3–O1, T5–O1 and their right-sided pairs.
  • Reactivity. Alpha attenuates when the eyes open and returns about 0.3 s after they close, briefly faster (the “squeak”).
  • Eye movements. The cornea is positive. On closing and blinking, the eyes roll up (Bell’s phenomenon), so Fp1/Fp2 go positive: a downward deflection, red frontally on the map. In the average reference the blink is part of the average, so it also appears with opposite sign at posterior electrodes.
  • Normal limits (awake adult). PDR 8 Hz or faster; amplitude commonly 15–50 µV; an amplitude asymmetry over 50% or a frequency difference over 1 Hz is abnormal (the right side is often slightly higher in normal people). Unilateral failure to attenuate (Bancaud phenomenon) points to a lesion on the side that fails to attenuate.

Simulated signal, not patient data. Sources are current dipoles in a simplified spherical head with no skull layer, plus random background sources, so amplitudes and field shapes are approximate.

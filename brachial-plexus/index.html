Guide

Getting started

Pick a syndrome from the bar at the top, or click segments of the plexus to build your own lesion. Severity scales every lesion; radiculopathy presets start at 60%.

Mystery lesion picks a random syndrome and hides the lesion. Localise it from the strength, sensory, reflex and NCS/EMG findings, then press Reveal answer. Clicking the plexus or a syndrome ends the quiz.

If the lesions you click match a known syndrome, its name and description appear.

"Downstream" means every segment whose fibres pass through a lesion. A segment that also carries fibres from an intact root is only partly affected; for example, an upper trunk lesion partly affects the lateral cord because C7 still reaches it through the middle trunk. Hover a segment to see which roots it has lost. Below 100% severity every downstream segment counts as partly affected.

The long thoracic nerve (C5–7) and the medial root of the median nerve cross other lines; the gap in the crossed line shows which runs over the top.

Sensation

Deficit shows numb skin in dark grey. Dermatomes and Nerves colour the map by root or by cutaneous nerve; lost areas are hatched dark so the map stays readable. The shoulder cap (C4) is cervical plexus and the medial upper arm (T2, intercostobrachial) is outside the brachial plexus, so neither changes.

Muscles

Weak muscles carry their grade number, so colour is never the only cue. The grades darken steadily from 5 to 0, which keeps them in order in greyscale and for colour-blind viewers. Grey muscles are drawn for orientation but not graded. The trapezius is removed from the posterior view to show the rhomboids and spinati. Muscles that cannot be seen from the surface (subscapularis, supinator, EIP, EPL, FDP, FPL, pronator quadratus) are listed as chips under the figures.

Localisation clues

  • Paraspinals are supplied by the posterior rami, which leave at the root. Denervation means the lesion is at the root.
  • SNAPs: the sensory cell body sits in the dorsal root ganglion, outside the cord. A root lesion is proximal to the ganglion (preganglionic), so the SNAP stays normal even when the skin is numb. Plexus and nerve lesions are postganglionic and reduce the SNAP.
  • Level markers are muscles whose nerves leave the plexus at a known level: rhomboids (dorsal scapular, C5 root), serratus anterior (long thoracic, C5–7 roots), supraspinatus (suprascapular, upper trunk), clavicular pectoralis (lateral pectoral, lateral cord) and latissimus dorsi (thoracodorsal, posterior cord). If one is weak, the lesion is at or above where its nerve branches off. If it is spared, the lesion is below that branch or on a different path. The most proximal weak marker gives the highest level involved.
  • Sensory loss lists the dermatomes and cutaneous nerve territories that are numb, as a text version of the sensory map.

Reflexes, NCS and EMG

Root lesions sit proximal to the dorsal root ganglion, so SNAPs stay normal even in numb skin ("Normal (numb skin)"), while the cervical paraspinals denervate. Lesions in the plexus or beyond reduce SNAPs and spare the paraspinals.

Each SNAP is listed with the root that mainly carries it: LABC and median digit 2 test C6, superficial radial C6, ulnar digit 5 and dorsal ulnar cutaneous C8, MABC T1. Reflexes grade as 2+ normal, 1+ reduced or 0 absent.

Needle EMG lists every limb muscle with any loss of innervation, plus the paraspinals. Fibrillations take about 2–3 weeks to appear after injury; the simulator shows the established picture.

How the model works

  • Each muscle lists its roots with an approximate weighting and the nerve that carries each root. Every root's route runs root → trunk → anterior or posterior division → cord → nerve segments. A lesion anywhere on a route removes that root's share, and the remaining share sets the MRC grade. Root weightings follow standard teaching tables; real innervation varies between people.
  • Skin patches carry both a dermatome and a peripheral nerve, so root lesions give dermatomal loss and nerve lesions give nerve-territory loss from the same map. Dermatome borders overlap more in patients than drawn here, so a single root lesion usually causes less sensory loss than shown.
  • Median fibres from C6–C7 run in the lateral cord (sensory to the lateral digits, pronator teres, FCR); C8–T1 fibres run in the medial cord (hand muscles). That is why lower trunk lesions spare the median digit 2 SNAP.
  • Muscle outlines are schematic, drawn after standard atlas views of the superficial layer.
  • Not modelled: Horner syndrome with T1 involvement, C7 contributions to the ulnar nerve, Martin-Gruber and other anastomoses, pre- and post-fixed plexuses, conduction block versus axonal loss, APB-predominant wasting in thoracic outlet syndrome, FCU sparing in cubital tunnel syndrome, and the timing of EMG changes.

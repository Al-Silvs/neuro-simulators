    How the model works, and what it leaves out
    • Each CN VI nucleus fires at a resting tone and increases for gaze toward its own side. It drives the ipsilateral LR through CN VI and, through internuclear neurons that cross and ascend in the opposite MLF, the contralateral MR subnucleus.
    • The PPRF supplies the voluntary/saccadic horizontal command to the CN VI nucleus on its side. In VOR mode vestibular input reaches the CN VI nuclei directly, so PPRF lesions stop mattering and CN VI nucleus lesions do not.
    • Eye position is the net pull of agonist minus antagonist, plus the small horizontal actions of the vertical recti (adduct) and obliques (abduct). Vertical action is weighted toward the recti in abduction and the obliques in adduction; torsion the other way round.
    • Head tilt drives counter-roll: the eye on the tilted side intorts (SR + SO), the other extorts (IR + IO). Losing SO leaves the SR elevation unopposed, which gives the Bielschowsky sign.
    • A pontine MLF lesion leaves a small tonic input to MR, so primary position stays nearly aligned. A midbrain MLF lesion here also removes that tonic input and the convergence drive, which is one proposed explanation for WEBINO exotropia; the mechanism in patients is debated.
    • A CN III nucleus lesion weakens ipsilateral MR, IR, IO and both SRs (the SR subnucleus projects contralaterally and its crossing fibres pass through the other nucleus), with bilateral ptosis from the shared central caudal nucleus.
    • Not modelled: vertical gaze centres (riMLF, INC), skew deviation, saccade main-sequence detail, mechanical restriction, Listing's law. A CN VI nucleus lesion leaves the eyes resting deviated away from the lesion; the model probably overstates that drift. Degrees and prism dioptres are qualitative, not calibrated to patient data.

How to read it
  1. Every 7 s the cortex sends a command into the striatum.
  2. D1 cells briefly silence GPi/SNr, the thalamic gate opens, motor cortex fires and the arm reaches the target.
  3. Between commands GPi/SNr keeps the gate shut, so the arm stays still.
  4. Pick a disease: the orange ring marks where it starts; ▲/▼ show the knock-on changes compared with normal.

Adjust the circuit

This is a simplified rate model of the classic box-and-arrow scheme (Albin, Young and Penney 1989; DeLong 1990). Activity is in arbitrary units and timing is slowed down for visibility. The model leaves out beta oscillations and tremor, striatal interneurons, thalamostriatal and GPe–striatal feedback, and the overlap between D1 and D2 pathways, so treat it as a teaching sketch rather than a physiological model.

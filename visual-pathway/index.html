How the model works, and what it leaves out
  • Every point in each eye's field is traced along its fibres: nasal retina (temporal field) crosses at the chiasm, so the left hemifield of both eyes reaches the right hemisphere. Upper field travels in the inferior retina, Meyer's loop and the lingual gyrus; lower field in the superior retina, the parietal radiation and the cuneus. The macula is represented at the occipital pole and the far temporal periphery at the anterior calcarine cortex.
  • The temporal crescent is the part of one eye's temporal field that the other eye's nasal field does not reach. It is drawn from the field extents used here: 90° temporal, 60° nasal, 60° up, 70° down.
  • Pupillomotor afferents leave the tract before the LGN, so only lesions up to and including the tract change the RAPD. Input is weighted toward fixation. A tract lesion gives a mild RAPD in the contralateral eye, whose larger temporal hemifield is lost.
  • The junctional scotoma uses the classic explanation (inferonasal crossing fibres looping forward into the opposite optic nerve, Willebrand's knee). Whether that loop exists in vivo is disputed; the field pattern itself is well described.
  • Lesions are complete within their territory. Partial lesions, incongruity of tract and temporal-lobe lesions, field depth, glaucomatous arcuate defects and functional (tubular) fields are not modelled.
